*The speech below is satire. The events it riffs on are real and listed with sources at the bottom. The jokes are jokes; the imaginary office dialogue is mine.*

One story today. Anthropic has put Claude in meetings. We spent years asking whether artificial intelligence would understand the human condition, and now we are making it attend a planning call. This feels less like a breakthrough and more like community service.

The announcement is called Claude Meetings. According to Anthropic, as reported by Reuters, you can add Claude to Google Meet as a visible participant. It follows the discussion, drafts plans, creates timelines and answers questions in the chat. The ambition is to start doing the work while the meeting is still happening. A dangerous precedent. Some of us were relying on the meeting to delay the work until Thursday.

Until now, the respectable meeting assistant took notes. It documented the conversation, produced action items and left us to arrange a second meeting about why none of them had happened. Claude is trying to collapse that entire ecosystem into one call. Somewhere, a recurring calendar invitation has begun to sweat.

There is a practical example in the coverage: the assistant can retrieve figures from connected work apps while people talk. Imagine saying you will have to get back to everyone with last year's numbers, and then the numbers arrive. Immediately. In the chat. In front of your manager. We were promised that AI would remove friction. Nobody explained that some of the friction was load-bearing.

Anthropic also announced Whiteboards and Sheets. The board can become an organized plan; the spreadsheet can have tabs, formulas and dropdowns. This is excellent news for anyone who wanted a working spreadsheet, and a difficult morning for anyone whose principal contribution was suggesting that somebody should make one.

To be clear, those are announced capabilities, not my independent verdict on how reliably the beta does them. Claude Meetings is starting on Team and Enterprise plans through Google Meet. Your particular meeting may still defeat it. I have attended discussions where a timeline was requested before anyone had established what the project was. No benchmark prepares a model for that kind of enterprise reasoning.

TechCrunch reports one especially human feature: without admin authorization for external guests, Claude will leave when an outside guest joins. A machine has acquired the ability to leave a meeting under a clearly defined policy. Meanwhile, the humans are still waiting for someone senior to say they have a hard stop.

That's today. The new colleague can draft the plan before the call ends, which means we may soon have to find out whether the plan was actually what we wanted. The technology is in beta. Our ability to agree on the scope remains experimental.

## The facts behind the jokes

Anthropic announced Claude Meetings on 10 November. Reuters reports that it adds Claude as a visible participant in Google Meet, with announced abilities to follow a discussion, draft plans, create timelines and answer questions in chat. It is launching in beta for Team and Enterprise customers, initially on Google Meet ([Reuters via The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/technology/artificial-intelligence/anthropic-brings-claude-to-google-meet-to-tackle-tasks-live-10958395/), [Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/anthropic-brings-claude-to-google-meet-to-tackle-tasks-live-5016423)). TechCrunch describes connected-app information retrieval, Whiteboards and Sheets, and reports that Claude identifies itself in the call and chat. Without admin authorization for external guests, it will not join such calls and will leave if an external guest joins ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/10/anthropic-launches-claude-meetings-to-turn-workplace-discussions-into-plans-and-tasks/)). Sources checked the morning of 11 November. Product descriptions are attributed announcements, not independently tested performance claims.
