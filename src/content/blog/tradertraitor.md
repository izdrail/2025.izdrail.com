*The speech below is satire. The events it riffs on are real and listed with sources at the bottom. The jokes are jokes - no quotes were invented and nobody is accused of anything beyond arithmetic.*

One story today: what appears to be the biggest crypto heist on record, and the suspect list has one country on it. Facts from the FBI, AP, Reuters and the BBC; the balaclava is mine.

Someone has stolen more than six billion dollars from Binance, the biggest crypto exchange on Earth, in what AP says appears to be the largest crypto heist on record. The FBI has now formally named a suspect, and it is North Korea: a country famous for having almost no internet. One of the poorest nations in the world just pulled it off. They have three computers, and all three of them are loaded.

The previous record, by the way, was also North Korea's: $1.5 billion from the Bybit exchange last year. So congratulations to Pyongyang on beating Pyongyang. Reporting says the state recruits its most promising university students and channels them into hacker units. Somewhere there is a careers fair with one booth, and the booth is wearing a mask.

My favourite part is the response. The FBI has published more than 600 Ethereum addresses, 19 Bitcoin ones and one extremely lonely Solana address, and asked the world's node operators, DeFi services and exchanges to please block them. Fifteen years of building a decentralised, censorship-resistant financial system that no one can control, and the emergency plan is everyone agreeing to control it together, quickly, ideally by the weekend.

To be fair, the recovery effort is moving: Binance says it has already replaced 90% of the stolen tokens using company money and promises to cover the rest, so users will not bear the loss, which is genuinely impressive and also means somebody's treasury just had the worst weekend of its life. The company's story has also evolved from "one engineer's laptop" to "complex attack" with "additional points of breach", so the incident review is going great. Meanwhile the thieves are converting the lot into ETH, and Chainalysis says they have "too much money for their trusted laundering venues to handle", which is a customer segment I did not know existed.

That's today: the world's most offline country is accused of what appears to be the biggest crypto heist ever, and the authorities are responding with a strongly worded list of wallet addresses. The facts are the FBI's, AP's, Reuters' and the BBC's; the balaclava is mine. Sources checked the morning of 2 Nov.

## The facts behind the jokes

The FBI, with the Secret Service and IRS-CI, said on 31 October that North Korea was responsible for the theft of more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or about 29 October - activity it tracks as TraderTraitor, a subunit of the Lazarus Group - and that the stolen assets are being converted primarily into Ethereum ahead of laundering through DeFi and instant swap services; the alert lists the linked addresses (608 ETH, 19 BTC and one SOL) and urges node operators, instant exchanges, DeFi services and other crypto firms to block them ([FBI cyber alert](https://www.fbi.gov/investigate/cyber/alerts/2026/north-korea-responsible-for-6-billion-binance-attack); address counts also [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/10/31/fbi-confirms-north-korea-s-role-in-usd6-billion-binance-hack-asks-defi-community-to-block-btc-eth-sol-addresses)). AP's estimate is $6.3 billion, "what appears to be the largest cryptocurrency heist on record" ([AP](https://apnews.com/article/binance-crypto-hack-north-korea-fbi-883f18140c1e21ce0c38781e72cbe315)). TRM Labs and Chainalysis had identified the group "with high confidence"; the attackers appear to have gained access to Binance's hot-wallet signing system, with groundwork laid at least 36 hours before the theft and most of the money gone in about 20 minutes ([BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/ck9dz3v7z7v3o); [Cybersecurity Dive](https://www.cybersecuritydive.com/news/binance-hack-north-korea-cryptocurrency-theft/873304/)). Pyongyang has long denied funding itself through cybercrime (BBC). Binance's account evolved from a single engineer's compromised laptop to a "complex attack" with "additional points of breach", and the company says it has replaced 90% of the stolen amount using company money and committed to covering the rest, so users will not bear the loss - company-reported ([Reuters via NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/business-news/fbi-says-north-korea-was-6-billion-binance-crypto-hack-rcna606758)). Stablecoin issuers froze over $2.4 billion of the funds, and Chainalysis expects "tens of thousands" of laundering addresses as the money moves on ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/10/31/fbi-confirms-north-korea-s-role-in-usd6-billion-binance-hack-asks-defi-community-to-block-btc-eth-sol-addresses)).
