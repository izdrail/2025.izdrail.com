*The piece below is satire. The events it riffs on are real and listed with sources at the bottom. The jokes are jokes - the allegations remain allegations, no quotes were invented, and no CEOs were reinstated in the writing of this column.*

Earnings day arrived, as it always does, regardless of the ongoing internal drama. Nvidia reported its quarterly figures today, a predictable event that managed to overshadow the considerable organisational upheaval swirling around its departed founder. One observes the corporate governance spectacle unfolding, a performance so meticulously staged it borders on high-fantasy. We have a petition boasting 180,000 signatures, a figure up from 165,000 the previous day, signalling a certain level of sustained, if ultimately performative, public sentiment. Simultaneously, a prediction market, Kalshi, assigns Huang a 14 per cent chance of returning as CEO, trailing only Arm CEO Rene Haas, with analyst Gene Munster placing his estimate at 51 per cent.

The supporting cast of this restoration drama is assembling. There is a 'war room' campaign underway, a gathering of Silicon Valley executives intent on lobbying for a comeback and courting the White House. This is not mere management; it is a full-blown restoration, complete with its own courtly intrigue. The board, meanwhile, has opted for the most prosaic solution for deciding the future: a CEO search firm. To add further flavour to this unfolding monarchy-esque narrative, law firm Wachtell Lipton has been retained to lead an investigation into Huang's conduct, examining potential compromises to national security or company secrets. Even President Trump has offered a nod of approval, stating he would certainly not mind seeing that.

The narrative is rich, the scaffolding of corporate drama sturdy, if utterly pointless. We are watching a highly specialised, high-stakes fandom surrounding a former titan, where the mechanisms of governance are being treated with the earnestness usually reserved for dynastic succession. It is all very theatrical, a grand production staged against the backdrop of a slightly depressed Nasdaq, which dipped 2.5 per cent today. The entire enterprise feels less like serious business and more like a protracted, high-budget historical reenactment of a royal family drama.

Ultimately, the only entities demonstrably engaged in the actual hiring process appear to be the search firm and the law firm. The rest is just the supporting cast, the petitions, the prediction markets, and the lobbying efforts, all circling the central, unresolved question.

## The facts behind the jokes

Nvidia's board is not considering bringing Jensen Huang back as CEO and is focused on an investigation into him, a person familiar told CNBC on 16 November; the company has retained law firm Wachtell Lipton to probe whether his conduct compromised national security or company secrets, and on the Friday before, a board representative said it was selecting a CEO search firm. Huang resigned last week after 33 years as CEO, days after disclosing an affair and following reports of others (CNBC). The reinstatement petition stood at 180,000 signatures with backing from Elon Musk, Garry Tan and Tim Sweeney, up from 165,000 the day before (CNBC, Guardian). The Financial Times reported on Sunday that Huang is considering a comeback and that an ally is assembling a "war room" of Silicon Valley executives to lobby for him and court the White House; President Trump said on Monday, "I would certainly not mind seeing that" (Axios). Kalshi traders gave Huang a 14 per cent chance of becoming the next permanent CEO, second behind Arm's Rene Haas and up from about 8 per cent on Sunday, while analyst Gene Munster put the figure at 51 per cent; governance expert Charles Elson called a return exceedingly unlikely: "There was a very good reason for him leaving - that reason hasn't changed" (Axios). Nvidia reports quarterly earnings today, Tuesday 17 November (ABC News, Yahoo Finance); the turmoil knocked 2.5 per cent off the Nasdaq and left Bitcoin near $65,000 (Bitcoin Magazine).
