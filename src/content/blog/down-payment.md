*The piece below is satire. The events it riffs on are real and listed with sources at the bottom. The jokes are jokes - no quotes were invented, and no opening bells were harmed in the writing of this column.*

It is a remarkable thing, this modern financial architecture. We have reached a point where the sheer scale of speculative commitment requires a new metric for valuation. Consider the recent offering from Anthropic, ticker ANT. The company priced its initial offering at $83 per share on Wednesday, a figure set above the preliminary range of $70 to $78 established the previous week. On Thursday morning, Dario Amodei and the six co-founders ring the opening bell on Nasdaq, as is customary.

The arithmetic underpinning this event is rather diverting. The record raise totals $81.3 billion, set against an $80 billion net loss recorded in the first half of 2026. The market, in effect, has refinanced the first half of the year with $1.3 billion to spare. Investors have clearly decided that the largest IPO on record, edging past SpaceX's June debut, is merely a down payment on the electricity bill.

The compute commitments are staggering. We are looking at more than $70 billion in commitments over the next twelve months, escalating to over $450 billion by 2030 and another $450 billion by 2045. It is a peculiar juxtaposition: the largest IPO on record being framed as a mere deposit against future energy consumption. The sheer magnitude of the forward-looking expenditure dwarfs the immediate revenue figures, which saw $41 million in 2023 and $386 million in 2024, before jumping to $4.585 billion in 2025 and then reaching $16.286 billion in the first half of 2026 alone.

The total capital raised as of the end of June reached $141.1 billion. This volume, alongside the 980 million shares issued, solidifies the status of this event. Furthermore, the co-founders are also selling. Amodei and the six fellow founders each plan to sell about $260 million worth of shares at the offering, while their individual stakes are valued at more than $18 billion apiece. The transaction is tidy, once you ignore the underlying absurdity of equating speculative AI capital with utility payments.

Meanwhile, the broader industry exhibits a similar cautious approach. OpenAI, for instance, has elected to postpone its own IPO, citing a need to overcome safety concerns before proceeding. This suggests a shared industry reluctance to fully commit to the next phase of deployment. Anthropic's debut, occurring amid an AI slowdown and one week after the Nvidia CEO situation, simply underscores the current environment. It is a clear demonstration that in this sector, the mechanism of going public is less about immediate profitability and more about securing the necessary collateral for future infrastructural demands. The ticker ANT will undoubtedly be a footnote in the next decade of infrastructure financing.

## The facts behind the jokes

Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 per share on Wednesday, above the preliminary range of $70 to $78 set the previous week; trading begins Thursday 19 November on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT, with CEO Dario Amodei and co-founders ringing the opening bell (Guardian, AP, 18-19 Nov). The raise is a record $81.3 billion at a valuation of about $1.5 trillion, making it the biggest IPO on record, surpassing SpaceX's June debut (NPR, AP, Reuters via investing.com). The offering covers 980 million shares, about 956 million of them sold by the company (AP). Per the prospectus figures reported by the Guardian: revenue was $41 million in 2023, $386 million in 2024, $4.585 billion in 2025 (up more than 1,000 per cent) and $16.286 billion in the first six months of 2026; the net loss for that half-year was $80 billion, and total capital raised as of the end of June stood at $141.1 billion. Compute purchase commitments and leases exceed $70 billion over the next 12 months, $450 billion by 2030 and a further $450 billion by 2045 (Guardian). Amodei and six fellow co-founders each plan to sell about $260 million worth of shares in the IPO; at the top of the range their stakes were worth more than $18 billion each (Guardian). OpenAI has delayed its own IPO, Sam Altman having said the company opted to wait until it can overcome safety concerns (Guardian). The pricing lands one week after Anthropic had to send bankers a corrected version of its IPO figures, which this column covered on 10 November as "Minor inaccuracies". Anthropic, founded in 2021, is the first company at the forefront of the generative AI boom to go public, and it does so amid an AI slowdown and a week after the Nvidia CEO scandal (Guardian, AP).
