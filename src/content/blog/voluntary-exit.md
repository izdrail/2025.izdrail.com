*The speech below is satire. The events it riffs on are real and listed with sources at the bottom. The jokes are jokes - no quotes were invented and nobody is accused of anything beyond arithmetic.*

One story today, because only one mattered. Facts from Microsoft's own blog and investor release; the packing boxes are mine.

Microsoft has invented a new genre of corporate communication: the resignation invitation. On Monday it emailed 60,000 people - more than a quarter of the company - to ask, warmly, whether they had considered a future outside the company. Decisions are due in early December. Leavers go on 31 December, which is one way to keep the Christmas party numbers manageable.

The memo itself is a masterpiece of the form. Leaving is called "shaping your next chapter". Some teams are offering the exit and some are not, which must do wonders for morale in both. And the whole thing closes with a line I will treasure forever: "This Viva Engage post has been edited slightly for external use." I would pay a small fortune for the unedited-slightly version.

Two days later, Microsoft announced a $92 billion quarter: revenue up 19%, profit up 32%, cloud up 30%. Business, by the company's own numbers, is booming, which is exactly why 60,000 people have until early December to decide whether they'd like to stop being part of it.

To be fair, there is a real story under the wordplay: the company is spending more than $50 billion a quarter on AI data centres, and the money has to come from somewhere, and it turns out somewhere is payroll-adjacent. The money bonfire from Day One now has a facilities team. I just enjoy that the official position is that this is a gift of choice. It is a gift. You have until December to accept it, and HR will know if you don't.

That's today: Microsoft put up huge numbers and celebrated by inviting a quarter of its staff to go and shape their next chapter somewhere else. Sources checked the morning of 29 Oct: Microsoft's official blog for the offer, CNBC for the numbers, and Microsoft's own investor release for the quarter. The facts are theirs; the memo was edited slightly.

## The facts behind the jokes

Microsoft introduced the Voluntary Exit Offer on 26 October for eligible employees at level 70 and below in the US, Canada, India and the UK, promising "financial support and career planning resources", and noting that the internal post "has been edited slightly for external use" ([Microsoft's official blog](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/10/26/introducing-a-voluntary-exit-offer/)). Around 60,000 people are eligible - 27% of the 223,000 workforce - with decisions due in early December and exits on 31 December; April brought Microsoft's first-ever voluntary retirement program (about 7% of US staff eligible) and July 4,800 layoffs, while September-quarter capital spending was guided above $50B, mostly AI data centres ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/26/microsoft-msft-voluntary-buyouts.html), citing a person familiar). Two days later Microsoft reported Q1 FY2027 results: revenue of $92.3B, up 19%, net income of $36.6B, up 32%, and Microsoft Cloud revenue of $63.9B, up 30% ([Microsoft investor relations](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2027-q1/press-release-webcast)). The Wall Street Journal's headline, for the connoisseurs: "Just Don't Call It a Layoff" ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/business/microsoft-job-cuts-buyout-offers-ff3c5dde)).
