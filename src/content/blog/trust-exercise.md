*The speech below is satire. The events it riffs on are real and listed with sources at the bottom. The jokes are jokes - no quotes were invented, and every claim about Anthropic is attributed where it appears.*

An independent lab spent eleven weeks inside Anthropic and concluded its AI progress has roughly doubled. It can tell you that. It just cannot show you why.

*Illustration generated for this post. The elephant is fine. It read the report. Most of it was black bars, but the bits between the bars were very reassuring.*

Eleven weeks. Four auditors. Unprecedented access to Anthropic's training metrics, internal models, and staff. At the end of it, METR produced a roughly one-hundred-page report whose most informative evidence you are not allowed to see. The conclusion is public. The receipts are confidential. It is the research equivalent of a restaurant hanging a sign that says the kitchen was inspected and the inspector has feelings.

To its credit, METR is admirably upfront about the arrangement. It published a table of what it saw versus what it may say. Algorithmic progress estimates: seen, cannot say. Key intermediate measures: seen, cannot say. Bottleneck details: partly seen, cannot say. It reads like a menu where every dish is marked available, not for you. Anthropic's stated reason is that the details could help rival labs move faster, which is a perfectly rational position that also happens to be indistinguishable from "the dog ate my homework, competitively."

The most scientific instrument in the entire report is a Slack poll. Anthropic staff vote with emoji reactions on how much faster the newest model makes them, and in October the self-reported speedup hit 12x. METR itself flags this as low-effort consideration, which is fair, because low-effort consideration is also how the votes were cast. Somewhere a researcher tapped a rocket emoji, and that tap is now load-bearing evidence in the debate over whether the rockets are multiplying too quickly.

The genuinely eerie part is phrased like a nature documentary. METR says researchers describe "signs of life" that fully autonomous AI research contributions could arrive soon, which is the exact wording NASA reserves for microbes on Mars, here applied to the thing whose progress was being audited. And the one component of the engagement Anthropic cancelled midway was the verification of how its compute splits between capabilities and safety. To keep the work focused, Anthropic said. Focused. On anything else.

Meanwhile, per the AP, the company is preparing to go public while its CEO warns about the very pace of improvement his company is reportedly doubling. Depending on which document you open, twice the speed is either a risk disclosure or a growth metric. The market will get to price it. The public does not get to check it. Everyone involved agrees this is fine, and they have the black bars to prove it.

## The facts behind the jokes

- **The report.** METR, an independent AI evaluation nonprofit, published an assessment of AI-driven R&D acceleration at Anthropic after eleven weeks of high-access review, running 12 August to 29 October (METR).
- **The headline.** Anthropic is improving its models on well-specified tasks at roughly twice its March 2024 to October 2025 rate, though METR says it would not be surprised if the true figure were 1.3x or 3x (METR).
- **The hard number.** Internal benchmark scores (AECI) accelerated 1.9x since October 2025, with a 95% confidence interval of 1.5x to 2.8x; depending on methodology, METR's robustness checks run from 1.1x to 2.9x (METR).
- **The cause.** METR guesses about 80% of the current acceleration comes from Anthropic using its own AI, and says it is confident the acceleration did not come from scaling training compute (METR).
- **The instrument.** One line of evidence is a Slack poll where staff use emoji reactions to vote their speedup from the latest model; it reached 12x in October, which METR itself describes as low-effort consideration (METR).
- **The catch.** Anthropic redacted a majority of the evidence METR found most informative, describing the redactions as "aimed at removing confidential information that could accelerate other AI developers" (METR, quoting Anthropic).
- **The backdrop.** The report lands as Anthropic prepares for its Wall Street debut, and while its CEO publicly calls for slowing the pace of AI improvement (AP).
- **Primary.** METR, "Independent Assessment of AI R&D Acceleration at Anthropic in October 2026" - [https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment/](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment/)
- **Corroboration.** Associated Press, "Anthropic is rapidly accelerating AI development amid slowdown calls, outside research group says" - [https://apnews.com/article/anthropic-metr-ai-claude-acceleration-9cf8c590ffc1abdb489179bdccc69c23](https://apnews.com/article/anthropic-metr-ai-claude-acceleration-9cf8c590ffc1abdb489179bdccc69c23)

The facts above are METR's, reported about Anthropic with Anthropic's redaction pen resting on the informative parts; the backdrop is the AP's. The elephant's opinions are its own. Satire aside, METR's uncertainty bands are wide and it says so itself.
