*The speech below is satire. The events it riffs on are real and listed with sources at the bottom. The jokes are jokes - no quotes were invented and nobody is accused of anything beyond arithmetic.*

One story today, because it contains multitudes. Facts from Anthropic's own announcement and a Reuters exclusive; the calculator is mine.

Monday was a big day at Anthropic. In the morning the company unveiled Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2, models built for "the most daunting technical challenges". To prove it, Anthropic listed what the models got up to in prerelease testing: they designed three working electronic devices from scratch, solved two open problems in the physics of black holes, and formalised the proof of Fermat's Last Theorem in 30 hours. Fermat famously could not fit his proof in a margin. Claude did it before lunch.

Then, late the same Monday, Anthropic had to send a second email. Hours earlier it had circulated its IPO filing to bankers: about 867 million shares at 70 to 80 dollars each, a raise of roughly 69 billion dollars. The follow-up explained that these figures were, and I am quoting the company here, "inaccurate". Revised numbers would follow.

I want to sit with this for a moment. The same organisation, on the same Monday, announced a machine that settles 350-year-old mathematics and withdrew a price range. Black hole physics: solved, two open problems, before lunch. How many shares at what price: pending, check back later, it's harder than it looks.

To be fair, there is a reasonable explanation, and Bloomberg Law found people willing to call it a "clerical misstep", a phrase doing an enormous amount of work in that sentence. A prospectus is long. Numbers drift. Any of us could accidentally type a fundraising target larger than the GDP of Denmark and hit send to every bank on the planet. It happens. Usually not to companies whose entire product is being extremely careful about tokens, but it happens.

And the stakes are considerable. CNBC reports Anthropic could seek a valuation of 1.5 to 2 trillion dollars and raise as much as 100 billion, on revenue of 16.3 billion. At those multiples, precision matters, which is presumably why the company retracted the figures instead of rounding them. You can't be too careful. Except, briefly, on Monday.

There is also a lovely product detail buried in the announcement. Fable 5.2 comes in two speeds: normal, and a "fast mode" that generates tokens up to 2.5 times quicker for double the price. Anthropic's IPO process appears to have shipped with fast mode enabled by default.

That's today: the people who taught a machine to be right about Fermat sent Wall Street a number, then sent a correction. The model is available now at 8 dollars per million input tokens. The accurate filing is available later. Sources checked the morning of 10 Nov: Anthropic's announcement for the model, Reuters for the retraction, Bloomberg Law for the clerical misstep, CNBC for the valuation maths. The facts are theirs; the figures were inaccurate.

## The facts behind the jokes

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2 on 9 November, describing them as built for "the most daunting technical challenges", with prerelease credits including three working electronic devices designed from scratch, two open problems in black hole physics solved, and the proof of Fermat's Last Theorem formalised in 30 hours; pricing is 8 dollars per million input tokens and 40 per million output, with an optional fast mode up to 2.5 times quicker at double the price ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2)). Hours later, Reuters reported that Anthropic had circulated IPO details to banks - roughly 867 million shares at 70 to 80 dollars each, a raise of nearly 70 billion dollars - before retracting them late on Monday, saying the pricing was inaccurate and that it would update the filing ([Reuters via CNA](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/business/exclusive-anthropics-nearly-70-billion-ipo-plan-briefly-circulated-banks-then-retracted-6537751), [Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/anthropic-retracts-ipo-details-after-sending-filing-to-bankers--reuters-5013961)). Bloomberg Law described the episode as a "clerical misstep" in which a prospectus "mistakenly dangled a $69.3 billion fundraising target" ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/capital-markets/anthropic-seeks-to-raise-69-3-billion-via-its-ipo-reuters-says)). CNBC TV18 reports Anthropic could seek a valuation of 1.5 to 2 trillion dollars, raise as much as 100 billion, and posted revenue of 16.3 billion ([CNBC TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/market/anthropic-ipo-target-document-sent-then-withdrawn-live-stream-event-valuation-price-reports-20030830.htm)); CNBC covered the model launch separately ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/09/anthropic-claude-fable-5-2-mythos-5-2.html)).
