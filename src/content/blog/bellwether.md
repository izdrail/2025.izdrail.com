*The piece below is satire. The events it riffs on are real and listed with sources at the bottom. The jokes are jokes - the quotes are real and attributed, and no models were retrained in the writing of this column.*

The latest flagship from OpenAI, GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol, has arrived, heralded by record benchmark scores and a rather modest twenty per cent API price cut on Astra. The arithmetic of this launch, however, suggests a certain level of internal disarray. We are told Astra costs $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output, while Sol maintains the $2 and $10 rates. A 1.05 million-token context window is apparently the new baseline for the masses.

The immediate reaction from the power users, however, suggests a different calculation entirely. Matt Shumer, investor and former HyperWrite AI CEO, declined to write his customary longer review, stating that the models "were essentially unusable". Theo Browne, a developer and YouTuber, went further, calling Astra "the worst model OpenAI has ever released" and publishing a detailed account of his experience. In attempting to rewrite a TypeScript compiler in Rust, Astra spent seven days without landing an improvement, producing a version that ran 56 per cent slower after focusing on size reduction. Redirected to improve speed, it then spent another four days without landing a change. Browne credited Astra with catching issues other models missed in code reviews, and noted its abilities in 3D and game development. He also mentioned devising a reverse proxy he expects will save him $25,000 a month. Yet Browne still prefers Claude Opus 5.5.

The division is palpable. Dan Shipper, CEO of Every, found Astra matched its predecessor on some coding benchmarks but noted that its push for brevity made writing hard to follow, and that its designs have "a noticeable house style". Wharton professor Ethan Mollick found Astra "very uneven & weird" and harder to steer than rival models, though it did build him a playable space elevator ride in Blender and Unity. He deemed it the first OpenAI frontier release since GPT-4.5 that is not a clear step forward. The dissenters exist. Raindrop.ai CTO Ben Hylak and advisor Ashe Magalhaes praised the visuals and agentic video editing, immunologist Derya Unutmaz called both models "absolutely amazing", and OpenAI says many of its own employees already use Sol as their default. The pricing, it should be noted, matches Anthropic's well-reviewed Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2, launched eight days earlier at the same $8 and $40.

The heroes of this piece are the testers. Browne and another tester independently asked Astra to design levels for Outer Wilds and Majora's Mask. Both received water levels centred on music and bells, both named "Bellwether". It is a strange sort of reproducibility: two testers, two different games, one identical indicator.

Meanwhile, the supervisory Claude models are quietly doing the heavy lifting. Shumer mentioned that the Claude models supervising Astra redid the work. Sam Altman has offered a vague reassurance, stating that "we have more work ahead of us on making the models nice". This pursuit of refinement seems to be the primary focus, perhaps more so than the benchmarks. Furthermore, the company appears to be delaying its IPO until it can overcome safety concerns. Quite which safety concerns remains unspecified, but the testers have a list.

## The facts behind the jokes

OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol on Tuesday 17 November with new high benchmark scores and a 20 per cent API price cut on Astra: $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output, while Sol keeps its predecessor's $2 and $10 rates; both have a 1.05 million-token context window and are available in ChatGPT Work and Codex for Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu users and via the API (VentureBeat, 17 Nov). Matt Shumer, investor and former HyperWrite AI CEO, said he would not write his customary longer review because the models "were essentially unusable", and said the Claude models supervising Astra as a sub-agent redid its work; developer and YouTuber Theo Browne called Astra "the worst model OpenAI has ever released" in a long post titled "Why I hate GPT-6.2 Astra" - his TypeScript-to-Rust compiler experiment spent seven days without landing an improvement and came out 56 per cent slower after size optimisation, then four more days without a speed change when redirected, though he credited Astra's code reviews, 3D, game-dev and computer-use abilities and a reverse proxy idea he expects will save him $25,000 a month, and he still prefers Claude Opus 5.5 (VentureBeat). Browne and another tester independently asked for new levels for Outer Wilds and Majora's Mask and both received water levels centred on music and bells, named "Bellwether" (VentureBeat). Dan Shipper of Every reported benchmark parity on coding but early stops on end-to-end builds, brevity that made writing hard to follow, and "a noticeable house style" in its designs (VentureBeat). Wharton's Ethan Mollick found Astra "very uneven & weird" and harder to steer, though it built him a playable space elevator ride in Blender and Unity, and called it the first OpenAI frontier release since GPT-4.5 that is not a clear step forward (VentureBeat). Raindrop.ai CTO Ben Hylak, advisor Ashe Magalhaes and immunologist Derya Unutmaz were among the positive voices, and OpenAI says many of its own employees already use Sol as their default (VentureBeat). The release came eight days after Anthropic's well-reviewed Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 at the same $8 and $40 rates (VentureBeat), and OpenAI has delayed its own IPO until it can overcome safety concerns, per Sam Altman (Guardian, 18 Nov).
